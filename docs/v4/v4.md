# ORCA V4

ORCA observes OpenRouter over time and turns captured evidence into useful retained knowledge.
The design favors product development and reinterpretation over minimizing every stored byte.

## Evidence and knowledge

- Scan artifacts preserve captured evidence. Extraction establishes useful entity observations;
  retained records need not reproduce upstream's duplicated and misleading entity nesting.
- Entity knowledge is cumulative; endpoint listing state evolves separately from retained values,
  following the [entity terminology](../../CONTEXT.md).
- Observation time dates our knowledge, not necessarily an upstream change. A baseline establishes
  knowledge at a real observation time; partial histories and gaps remain useful datasets.
- For fixed rules, supplied scans determine ingestion output. The earlier scan selects changes and
  the later scan dates their values; retained values stand independently of previous projections.

## One data strategy

1. **Artifacts preserve evidence.** They support inspection and revisiting extraction decisions.
2. **Extraction establishes entities.** It resolves known nesting, identities, relationships and
   naming once; downstream modules consume its output without understanding the upstream layout.
3. **Records preserve projectable observations.** Each entity and its related records retain the
   facts needed for later projection, including scan-derived extras and structural corrections.
4. **Extensible content is stored as JSON text.** Small ORCA-owned contracts use typed fields;
   arbitrary upstream content does not acquire database field-name or leaf-value restrictions.
5. **One shared lens assigns product meaning.** Required facts are deliberately validated; optional
   facts can be absent or invalid, and unknown content is preserved without assigning it meaning.
6. **Series preserve curated selections.** Pricing and readings directly serve history; selected
   structures survive storage without lossy metadata transformations.

## Modules contain complexity

- Each module conceals its mechanics behind a small interface. Callers depend on the output contract,
  rather than reconstructing the assumptions of the entire transform chain.
- Scan owns extraction; Projections owns interpretation. Storage encoding does not decide which
  upstream facts are meaningful, and individual products do not duplicate historical-schema handling.
- Ingestion moves forward through connected pairs. Current Catalog tables accumulate each pair's
  updates as ingestion phases; known entities persist and endpoints become unlisted rather than deleted.
- Capture, ingestion and event publication have separate completion meanings. Ingestion completion
  includes current Catalog updates and establishes the completed clock.
- Retained observations support later reinterpretation. Rebuilding current tables or historical series
  after a projection change is non-routine work; published events remain historical claims.
- Optimize the ordinary forward path. Add orchestration, synchronization and efficiency mechanisms
  when demonstrated needs justify them; preserve the ability to reinterpret retained facts.
